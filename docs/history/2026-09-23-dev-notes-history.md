### Repeated items get rays around the rail dot

- **Cause:** jay, pointing at a rail dot: "Can we use the tickling dot as repeated which is great." An item read a second time on a later done-day already records a pass, and the rail already prints the count as a numeral, but nothing about the dot said *repeated* at a glance.
- **Reasoning:** the dot is drawn by the generator with its status colour as an **inline** style, so a stylesheet cannot see that colour and a hard-coded ray colour would have made every repeat look the same regardless of done / important / revisit. The rays are therefore a `::before` conic-gradient ring reading a `--ray` custom property, and a small pass at load copies each dot's computed background into that property. Injected from `docs/topics/_progress.js` rather than edited into pages, for the same reason the status overlay loads there: ~780 generated pages share it, so site-wide behaviour has exactly one home. Checked first with Playwright that the dot had no existing animation to collide with (`animationName: "none"`).
- **Change:** `docs/topics/_progress.js` adds the ring, the 6s rotation, a radial mask so the dot itself stays clean, `prefers-reduced-motion` opt-out, and nudges the count numeral from `left:11px` to `left:15px` so it clears the ring. Commit `498d1ee6`, merged as `a88c8933`.
- **Result:** rays mean "read at least once more", the numeral means "how many". Verified in Chrome with forced values — `--ray` resolved to the item's own green, animation `nav-dot-rays` running. No item carries `data-times` yet, so nothing shows rays on the live site until a repeat is drained in.

### Tech #72: tokenized compute is a forward contract on a perishable good

- **Cause:** jay pasted one line — "JUST IN: BlackRock says AI compute could be tokenized in the future" — and asked for it as a Tech item. No URL came with it.
- **Reasoning:** restating the headline would have added nothing, so the card is built on what the headline hides. Compute is not becoming a new asset class; it is being **reclassified from a service into a financial claim**, and a GPU-hour is perishable, non-fungible and location-bound. Electricity and airline seats are the precedents, and both needed decades of contract engineering — because forward markets in perishables have never failed at issuance, they fail at deliverable grade, delivery point, preemption, and settlement on failure. The load-bearing word in BlackRock's framing is **collateral**: an asset manager cares that compute becomes financeable, but collateral implies liquidation, which here means finding someone to take physical delivery of GPU-hours they do not want at the worst possible moment. The card also lands where the section already is: BlackRock names x402 and MPP and then says permissions, identity and compliance are missing — the agent-payments card's argument one layer down, with the decimal point moved.
- **Change:** `docs/topics/pocs-tokenized-compute-is-a-forward-contract.html` at Tech #72 (type Brief, bin `deep`), with a claim-by-claim status table, the four contract terms, the join to the agent-payments card, three Jayverse landings and 18 key expressions. `raw/2026-09-23-tokenized-compute-is-a-forward-contract.md` records that the white paper itself was **not** read — only secondary coverage — and that the contract-design argument is the card's own, never to be attributed to BlackRock.
- **Result:** Tech 298 items, site total 943. The $1.1T-by-2030 figure is marked a projection, and BlackRock's stake in AI infrastructure through the AI Infrastructure Partnership is flagged as a reason to read the mechanics rather than the conclusion.
